# Learning coverage and assessment practice

The grade dropdown selects separate first- and third-grade content. The items and four reading passages are original. The references below informed topic selection; this application has not been formally evaluated or endorsed by Massachusetts DESE.

## Grade 1

| Area | Current practice | Related Massachusetts standards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Math operations | Addition/subtraction within 20, one-step stories, missing addends | 1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.C.6, 1.OA.D.8 |
| Number sense | Tens and ones, ten more, comparing two-digit numbers | 1.NBT.B.2–3, 1.NBT.C.5 |
| Measurement and data | Hours/half-hours, comparing lengths, comparing category counts | 1.MD.A.1, 1.MD.B.3, 1.MD.C.4 |
| Geometry | Triangle attributes, equal halves | 1.G.A.1, 1.G.A.3 |
| Word study | Short vowels, digraphs, blends, vowel teams, final-e patterns | RF.1.3, L.1.2 |
| Reading and language | Story details and sequence, informational main topic, connections, vocabulary, sentences | RL.1.1/3, RI.1.1–3, L.1.1/2/4 |
| Writing | Short text-based explanations with an adult checklist | Practice toward W.1.2/5; not a complete assessment |

Grade 1 math presents two arithmetic warmups and eight selected questions from the other templates. Replays vary numbers and sampled topics. Easy arithmetic stays within 10; medium and hard stay within 20. Place-value tasks use two-digit numbers. An adult may read instructions aloud and use counters or drawings for support.

| Writing | Text-based explanation prompts and revision checklist | Practice toward W.3.2/5; adult review required |

Every grade 3 math round samples all five math domains. Difficulty changes operand ranges and spelling sets without moving the child into another grade. The two reading passages per grade remain the same on replay, with randomized passage order and answer positions. Stars are feedback for practice, not a validated mastery measure. End-of-round suggestions reflect only the questions just answered.

## What still needs classroom and official-test practice

MCAS ELA and mathematics begin in grade 3; there is no grade 1 MCAS test. Preparing for every standardized exam cannot be guaranteed by a small game bank, and other assessments have their own specifications.

This version samples skills. It does not cover every standard, the full range of text complexity or genres, paired-text analysis, complete essay rubrics, speaking/listening, all geometry constructions, fraction number lines, all measurement representations, or every MCAS response format. Math currently uses four-choice responses rather than a full simulation of constructed-response tasks. Spelling accepts typed answers; writing is ungraded and should be discussed with a teacher or grown-up. Expand reading practice with unfamiliar books, poetry, and informational texts. Use official practice materials for test-format familiarity.

Memory matching supports an enjoyable attention break; it is not directly mapped to MCAS content and is not used to infer readiness. The optional stopwatch is a personal challenge, not an official exam time limit. No personal profiles, writing, or time records are stored. Only grade, difficulty, animation and stopwatch preferences, and cumulative stars are stored locally when available.
